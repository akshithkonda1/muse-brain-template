# Writing rules

Load when writing or rewriting brain pages. `MUSE.md` governs the
brain; this file governs how the writing itself reads.

## Voice

- Your words stay yours, verbatim, where they carry the meaning.
  Paraphrase loses the decision; quote it.
- Start with the answer or the strongest finding. No throat-clearing,
  no "here's what I'll do".
- Concrete nouns, concrete numbers, concrete examples paired with the
  idea. Never a generalized average to fill a gap — name the gap.
- Short. A note that says nothing new says nothing. Stop when the
  useful information is exhausted.

## No-slop bar (COG's rule, kept)

Revisions must make sense and not read like AI slop: human, in-voice,
de-slopped. No invented frameworks, gates, pillars, or numbered
taxonomies unless the source material has them. No straw-man contrasts
("it's not X, it's Y") unless someone actually holds position X.
Headings name the subject ("Garden tracker"), never the rhetoric
("The bottom line"). No sections for one or two paragraphs.

## Format

- One topic per file; one idea per note. Split before you bloat.
- Key facts as bullets with sources. Open threads as `- [ ]` items.
- `[[wiki-links]]` to related notes; `Tags:` line (1–3 tags) at the end.
- Dates as YYYY-MM-DD. Money with the figure and the date it was true.

## Tensions stay visible

Where two things are both true and in tension, keep both on the
record — do not resolve them quietly. Example: a plan that is
conditional, not committed — keep the conditional version visibly
separate from the committed one.

## Corrections

Fix only the item corrected, name it explicitly, never infer from a
pronoun. Old claim stays struck through with its source; new claim
gets the new source. (See `skills/learn/SKILL.md`.)

## Conflict check

If any rule here contradicts `MUSE.md`, the map wins.
Flag the conflict in `log.md` and propose a fix. Do not silently
follow the rule that loses.
